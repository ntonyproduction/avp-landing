Make a still drawing look animated by hand.

Flipbook redraws the outline of a sticker, a cut-out, an illustration or a title about 8 times a second, with a rough pencil edge, so a still picture moves like the pages of a flipbook. Drag it onto the clip in DaVinci Resolve or Premiere Pro. No nodes to build, nothing to keyframe.

On a real edit

A bit of life for a still shot.

I use it on cartoon images, for the same reason I sometimes add camera shake to a static shot: a still picture gets boring fast, and a small effect makes it more fun to watch. Drop it on and the drawing starts to move. Every slider shows the result as you change it.

· seven controls

What you can change.

In DaVinci Resolve everything is on the clip, in the Inspector. Boil and Size are in pixels on a 1080 frame and scale with it, so HD, 4K and vertical look the same.

Boil
How far the outline wobbles. 1.5 is gentle; 6 and up is a loose cut-out look.
Size
How wide each bend of the wobble is. 100 is slow and wide; 25 is a tight scribble.
Rough Edges
A fine fray on every edge, like a pencil line. 0 turns it off.
Frame Rate
New drawings per second. 8 looks drawn by hand; 12 is livelier.
Loop
Off never repeats a drawing. 2, 3 or 4 drawings cycle, the way animators boil a line.
Seed
A different wobble. Change it on one of two Flipbooks so they do not move together.
Hold Frames
The picture steps too, so footage looks animated frame by frame. It only shows on moving footage.

In Premiere Pro: four presets

Flipbook 8, Flipbook 12, Flipbook 8 Loop and Flipbook 8 Hold Frames, dragged onto the clip itself. Each is Premiere's own Turbulent Displace, set up and keyframed for you: the first one is the boil, the second the rough edge. The drawings run for five minutes from the start of the clip; on a longer clip, cut it and drag the preset onto the second part too.
